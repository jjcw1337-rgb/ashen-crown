# Nightly performance run, 2026-11-01

**Concept: store the model textures as lossy WebP (q0.9) instead of lossless.** It is a size concept, the first one
for the download-size goal in SIZE.md.

## What I profiled

- The game's own `--perf 12` on the fixed scenes: the moor (`--zone wild --grassspot`) at quality 1, 2 and 3, and the
  camp (`--zone town`) at quality 2, 1600x900 requested (the window came up at 1824x900), Apple M6, Forward+.
- The perf hook had no GPU numbers, so tonight it got them (`scripts/game.gd` `_perf_step`): GPU time per frame from
  `RenderingServer.viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu`, GPU busy % (GPU time / frame time), render-thread CPU
  time, engine start to zone ready, and video memory (total / textures / buffers). I ran the baseline again after
  adding them so the before numbers have them too.
- The contents of `.godot/imported` (the pack is essentially this folder plus the intro), and the export size for
  macOS and Windows (and Linux after).

## What the measurements showed

- `.godot/imported` was 602 MB, and 490 MB of it was the textures Godot extracts from the `.glb` files (249 files
  under `assets/models/`). Their `.import` files are git-ignored, so every checkout and CI makes them fresh with the
  texture importer's defaults: **compress/mode = Lossless**. A 2048² Meshy JPG of 0.5-3 MB became a 4-9.5 MB
  WebP-lossless `.ctex` (mipmaps included). Re-encoding a lossy JPG losslessly only stores its artefacts exactly.
- The 3D detection that would have switched them to VRAM compression never runs in a headless import, so they also
  sit uncompressed in video memory: the texture counter read **~1.0 GB at High and 1.9 GB at Ultra**.
- The frame is not GPU-bound: GPU busy is 57-66% in every scene (moor High: 25 ms GPU in a 43.6 ms frame). The rest
  goes on the main thread. The render thread's CPU is only 0.5 ms, so look at script and process time next.
- The camp draws **152 million primitives a frame** at High (the moor: 20 M) for 12.4 fps. That is the biggest frame
  cost found tonight; something there (grass in the camp? the Meshy props without LODs?) is worth a night.

## Hypothesis

Lossy WebP at quality 0.9 is visually lossless against sources that are already JPGs (the probe measured 41-42 dB
PSNR, and 39-41 dB on the two worst normal maps), and 5-8x smaller than lossless. Frame rate shouldn't change
(textures stay RGBA8 in VRAM either way). Load time might improve, since lossy WebP decodes faster than lossless.

| texture (2048²) | source JPG | lossless (before) | q0.7 | q0.8 | **q0.9** |

| plague coat_oxblood2 | 1031 KB | 4144 KB | 390 KB, 35.4 dB | 527 KB, 37.3 dB | **854 KB, 41.3 dB** |

## What changed (files)

- `project.godot`: `[importer_defaults] texture = {compress/mode: 1 (Lossy), compress/lossy_quality: 0.9,
  detect_3d/compress_to: 0}`. It only applies to textures **without** a committed `.import` file, which in this repo
  means exactly the textures under `assets/models/` (the glb-extracted ones and the NPCs' committed JPGs; every
  committed image elsewhere has its own `.import`, and `concepts/` is `.gdignore`d). Detection is off so an editor session can't flip them to VRAM behind our back.
- `assets/textures/plague/coat_*.jpg.import` (5 files): the Plague Doctor's coat dyes were the only committed
  JPG-sourced textures imported lossless (4.4 MB each) → lossy q0.9 (0.87 MB each).
- `scripts/game.gd`: the perf hook's GPU / memory / load-time lines.
- `CLAUDE.md`: the import default and how to refresh an old checkout.

I checked it the way CI does it: a fresh `git clone` and `godot --headless --import` gave all 249 extracted textures
`compress/mode=1`, and the import cache came out at 219 MB instead of 602 MB. Note: in 4.3, `--headless --editor
--quit` aborted the scan on a fresh clone here ("Scan thread aborted") and imported nothing; `--import` works. CI's
export step imports anyway.

\* Two more Medium runs after the change gave 1% lows of 30.7 and 36.3 fps (one even averaged 44 fps): the variance
between runs on this machine is bigger than the difference. Draw calls and primitives are unchanged.

- Load is **~1-1.4 s faster** (-16 to -27%) in every scene: lossy WebP decodes faster than lossless.
- Godot's texture-memory counter drops ~160-200 MB. I didn't confirm why. Likely some lossless imports kept an alpha
  channel or mip chain the lossy ones don't. Treat it as a reading of the monitor, not a measured GPU saving.

**Look.** Compared pixel by pixel on fixed screenshots (class screen, title, camp with three classes, the fort, the
camp tools' views): before → after differs by the same amount as two identical "after" runs differ from each other
(class screen 38.2 vs 38.8 dB, knight in the camp 39.5 vs 37.7 dB, fort 26.7 vs 26.4 dB; snow, embers and birds
move between runs). The strict test, the source JPG next to what the game now loads at 1:1 and 2x zoom, shows no
visible difference (knight armour, Plague coat). Images: `look_*_before_after.png`, `texture_*_src_vs_webp.png`.

**Tests:** playtest (all 15 stages), grass_test (all passed), coop_fx_test (0 failed), title_test, bg_test server +
client (0 failed both sides), mp_test host + join. Two things already fail on the untouched commit, so they aren't
from tonight's change: every `-s` tool prints `Compile Error: Identifier not found: Sfx` at load and then runs
normally, and mp_test's `[H4] partner pos ok=false` (the partner is more than 6 m from the host at that moment;
probably since the new WASD controls).

## What I'd try next

1. **The camp's 152 M primitives a frame**: find what draws them by switching parts off one at a time (grass, the
   Meshy camp props, trees, NPCs; the perf hook has no `--hide` option yet, so add one). It's likely a big frame-rate win at the camp (12 fps at High on an M6).
2. **Size, next steps**: the imported scenes (83 MB of meshes plus their automatic LODs: check mesh compression and
   whether the auto-LODs are worth their bytes), `knight_mocap.glb` and `knight_armored.glb` carrying the same three
   2048² textures byte for byte, the Mac pack holding both S3TC and ETC2 copies of every VRAM-compressed texture
   (Apple Silicon reads BC formats; verify before turning ETC2 off), 2048² textures on props that are never
   larger than a few hundred pixels on screen.
3. **VRAM compression instead of lossy** for the same textures, as a memory/bandwidth concept: ~4x less video memory
   (1 GB → ~250 MB) but bigger on disk than tonight's WebP (BC1 2.8 MB per 2048² with mips, twice on Mac). It conflicts
   with tonight's size win, unless only the largest textures get it.
4. **The main thread**: the GPU is idle ~40% of every frame. Time `_process` of Game, GrassField, the enemies and
   the hero to find where the other ~18 ms of the moor's frame goes.
